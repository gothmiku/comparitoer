Compare two videos locally, with Comparitoer

Comparitoer puts two video files on the same screen and the same clock. Check that their durations agree, drag the wipe divider to flip between the old and the new take, then play both in sync on one timeline. Everything runs in your browser: no uploads, no accounts, no backend.

How it works

  1. Drop video A and video B onto the two drop zones, or pick each one with the file dialog.
  2. Comparitoer reads both durations and reports whether they match within a tolerance you can adjust — 0.05 seconds by default.
  3. Drag the divider to compare frames, then play, scrub and step while both videos stay locked together.

What you can do

  • Duration check. A green verdict when the two clips match, an amber one with the exact difference in milliseconds when they do not, and a “compare anyway” option that clamps the timeline to the shorter video.
  • Wipe divider. Video A stays underneath, video B is clipped from the divider to the right edge, and the divider can be dragged by mouse, pen or finger.
  • One timeline. A is the master clock and B follows it: drift above 40 ms is corrected with a seek, smaller drift with a temporary 3% playback-rate nudge.
  • Independent mode. Give each video its own play button, timeline and frame step when the two clips should be inspected separately.
  • Audio. Mix both soundtracks, mute one side, or load a third local audio file to compare picture against a new mix.
  • Local conversion. If the browser cannot decode a file — ProRes, some HEVC and MKV files — Comparitoer re-encodes a temporary H.264 proxy on your machine.

Questions

Are my videos uploaded anywhere?

No. Comparitoer has no backend, no analytics and no runtime network requests. Each file is read straight from your disk into the browser and is released as soon as you replace it or close the page.

Which file formats work?

Anything your browser can play: H.264 or H.265 MP4, WebM, and the MOV files that Safari handles natively. Formats the browser rejects can usually be converted locally to an H.264 proxy inside the app.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Once the page has loaded there is nothing else to fetch, so no upload and no connection are ever needed to compare two files.

Is there a file size limit?

Playback has no artificial limit; the file is streamed from disk rather than read into memory. Only the optional local conversion has a size cap, because it holds a temporary copy of the result in browser memory.

What does it cost?

Nothing. Comparitoer is free, needs no account, and runs entirely in your browser.
